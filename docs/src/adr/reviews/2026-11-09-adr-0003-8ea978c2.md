## Prior blocker disposition

1. **Finding 4519328148 — Owner bypass: resolved.** Recovery step 1, ADR lines **141–144**, requires determining whether the compromised Deployer is also Owner. Step 2, lines **145–146**, expressly places Deployer revocation **after containment of compromised Owner authority**. If Owner cannot be secured or transferred, the procedure instead requires stopping use of affected children, disclosure, and feasible migration; it does not claim containment through role revocation.

2. **Dual-role revocation being skipped: resolved.** Step 2 requires revoking the compromised Deployer through `set_role` after Owner containment. It is not limited to accounts that were never Owner. Consequently, transferring ownership does not substitute for removing the compromised account’s explicit Deployer grant.

3. **Previously approved recovery safeguards: preserved.**
   - **Lines 147–148:** wait for pre-revocation deployment receipts and callbacks before enumeration and cleanup.
   - **Lines 149–150:** include children created after compromise and remove **every unauthorized key**, using trusted child full-access authority where available.
   - **Lines 151–152:** grant a reviewed replacement Deployer only after the key list is clean; replacement does not revoke child keys.
   - **Line 153:** verify the final key list and establish trusted child **code and state** before resuming.
   - **Lines 154–158:** disclose and migrate where feasible if integrity cannot safely be restored—even when a trusted key remains—or no authorized removal path exists. The keyless-market limitation remains explicit.

The comparison against approved candidate `6028d970` shows the Owner-containment correction and consequential renumbering, without removal of those safeguards. The explicit Uploader/Owner upload gate and pinned implementation-reference provenance also remain unchanged.

## Tradeoff assessment

The correction preserves the selected architecture: conventional Owner administration separate from explicit Uploader/Deployer membership; Borsh uploads funded through NEP-145; original-payer pending and retained charges; Owner hash-only approval; pending-only uniqueness; handle-moving migration; protocol reserves; shortfall-safe cleanup; and pre-/post-key-deletion DAO proofs.

It accurately retains the trust cost: a Deployer can install child full-access keys, and Owner retains broader authority than an explicit Deployer grant. Role separation therefore limits routine authority but does not make deployed children immutable or make RBAC revocation sufficient incident recovery.

Administrative withdrawal, forced unregistration, historical hash bans/indexes/scans, Owner→Admin migration, and entry-rewriting migration remain rejected. Production execution, gateway/tmplrmgr UX, human acceptance, and implementation merge remain deferred.

## Security and maintenance assessment

The pinned implementation supports the corrected ordering:

- `assert_role` at `lib.rs:191–197` accepts either the current Owner or explicit membership. Removing a Deployer grant cannot disable a still-compromised Owner.
- `set_role` at `lib.rs:199–229` changes explicit membership, independently of Owner authority. It supports last-member removal through `allow_removing_last_member:true`, also documented in the rollout procedure; granting a replacement first is unnecessary.
- `deploy` checks authority before constructing child-account actions and installs supplied full-access keys.
- The consuming callback, `deploy_01_finalize`, handles completion and accounting without rechecking the original caller’s role or removing child credentials. Previously dispatched work must therefore settle before cleanup is considered complete.

The recovery sequence now distinguishes **Owner containment, explicit-role revocation, asynchronous settlement, child-key cleanup, and child-integrity restoration**. It introduces no new recovery API or compatibility mechanism and does not invent registry-Owner authority to repair existing children.

## Blockers

1. **[P2] Revoke every unauthorized Deployer grant after Owner compromise**

   **File:** `docs/src/adr/0003-separate-registry-capabilities-and-uploader-funded-code-storage.md`  
   **Lines:** 145–148  
   **Confidence:** 0.94

   If the compromised Owner+Deployer used `set_role` to grant Deployer to another attacker-controlled account before containment, securing or transferring Owner and revoking only the original compromised Deployer leaves that additional grant active. The pinned implementation stores explicit membership independently of Owner (`lib.rs:186–219`), and `deploy` accepts any current Deployer (`lib.rs:633`), so the second account can dispatch new deployments after the prescribed settlement and child-key enumeration. Operators can therefore complete the documented recovery while the attacker retains deployment authority. **Minimum correction:** after securing Owner, enumerate explicit Deployer membership and revoke every unauthorized grant—not only the initially compromised account—before draining deployments and beginning child cleanup.
