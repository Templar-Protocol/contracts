## Prior blocker disposition

1. **Finding 4519026629—deployment authority surviving child-key cleanup: resolved.** Line **133** now expressly requires revoking the compromised Deployer through `set_role` **before any child-key removal**. Authority revocation is therefore a prerequisite, not a later consequence of rotating the operator.

2. **Post-compromise children and additional attacker-installed keys: covered.** Recovery must re-enumerate child keys, explicitly including **children created after the compromise**, remove **every unauthorized key**, rotate Deployer, and verify the final key list. It does not limit cleanup to the initially observed child or key.

3. **Attacker-modified child code/state: preserved as an independent gate.** Before resuming use, operators must establish trusted child **code and state**. Disclosure and migration to a newly reviewed market where feasible remain required if integrity cannot be established or safely restored, **even when a trusted child key survives**. A clean key list cannot substitute for integrity evidence.

4. **Unavailable removal authority and keyless-market limitations: preserved.** The stated direct-removal route requires a trusted full-access key **on that child**, if one remains. Where no trusted child key or authorized removal method exists, the same disclosure/migration fallback applies. The ADR neither invents registry-Owner authority over existing child keys nor promises that keyless markets can be patched.

5. **Migration, accounting, and rollout safeguards: unchanged.** Decision 4 retains handle-moving V1→V2 migration, historical bytes/prefixes and Owner-slot preservation, empty new accounting structures, and registry-funded historical code. Decision 5 retains protocol reserves, shortfall-safe failure cleanup, and atomic upgrade/migrate/solvency checking. Decision 6 still requires separate **pre- and post-registry-key-deletion** proofs before alpha→v1 promotion.

6. **Earlier authority and evidence-provenance corrections: preserved.** Owner exclusivity remains explicitly scoped to the contract API, with retained registry full-access keys disclosed as an independent authority path. Implementation evidence remains pinned to the separate immutable revision rather than presented as implementation in this branch.

## Tradeoff assessment

The correction changes incident sequencing, not the selected architecture:

- Conventional Owner administration—including two-step transfer and renunciation—remains separate from explicit Uploader/Deployer membership.
- Pre-funded Borsh uploads and original-payer pending/retained charges preserve payer ownership while acknowledging approval top-ups and protocol-owned backing.
- Owner hash-only approval retains legacy registration and callback compatibility.
- Pending-only uniqueness permits approved aliases without historical hash bans, indexes, or scans.
- Handle-moving migration avoids historical entry rewriting.

Administrative withdrawal, forced unregistration, Owner→Admin migration, and entry-rewriting migration remain rejected. Gateway/tmplrmgr UX, human acceptance, implementation merge, and production execution remain deferred.

## Security and maintenance assessment

The recovery model now separates three necessary outcomes: **stop compromised deployment authority, remove unauthorized child access, and establish trusted child code/state**.

That distinction matches the separate implementation. `deploy` checks `Role::Deployer`, then installs supplied keys through `add_full_access_key`; its finalization callback records deployment rather than managing child credentials (`contract/registry/src/lib.rs:624–789`). Registry-role revocation consequently does not remove already-installed child keys or repair prior code/storage modifications.

The existing `set_role` implementation provides the revocation path, including explicit permission to remove the last role member when needed; the supporting runbook documents those parameters. No new recovery API or universal restoration mechanism is assumed. Reusing existing authorization, approval dispatch, migration handles, and callback paths keeps the decision maintainable without concealing its operational trust requirements.

## Requirement coverage

- **Authority:** Conventional Owner behavior, including two-step transfer and renunciation, remains separate from explicit Uploader/Deployer membership. Contract-API guards remain distinguished from retained registry full-access-key authority.
- **Upload and accounting:** Borsh uploads use NEP-145 pre-funding. Pending and retained charges remain attributable to the original payer; released credit is payer-withdrawable. Approval top-ups and protocol-owned backing remain acknowledged.
- **Approval and compatibility:** Owner hash-only Uploaded/Global approval, legacy registration, permanent successful version keys, and retryable failed probes remain covered. Both approval variants have explicit consuming branches.
- **Uniqueness:** Duplicate rejection remains pending-only, permitting approved aliases without historical hash bans, indexes, or scans.
- **Migration and backing:** V1→V2 moves collection handles without rewriting historical entries or Owner slots. Protocol reserves, deployment-record reservations, shortfall-safe cleanup, and atomic V2 upgrade checking remain included.
- **Recovery and rollout:** Revocation precedes cleanup; all-key removal and trusted code/state are separate requirements. Pre- and post-key-deletion proofs remain mandatory.
- **Scope:** Production execution, gateway/tmplrmgr UX, human acceptance, and implementation merge remain deferred. Administrative withdrawal/forced unregister, Owner→Admin migration, and historical-entry rewriting remain rejected.

## Alternative fairness

The alternatives remain substantive and proportionate. Owner-only/deposit-based operation offers fewer interfaces but retains concentrated authority and less explicit retained-payer accounting. Replacing Owner with Admin adds migration without improving the selected capability separation. Historical hash bans/indexes change alias semantics and add history-management costs; forced unregistration/admin withdrawal conflict with payer-owned credit; entry rewriting increases migration risk unnecessarily.

The chosen design acknowledges its own costs: accounting complexity, payer top-ups, protocol-owned backing, and trusted deployment operators. The correction does not claim those trust costs disappear.

## Security, maintenance, and verification assessment

The recovery model now separates three concerns: **containing new deployment authority, removing existing unauthorized child credentials, and establishing child integrity**. Revocation does not itself remove child keys, repair state, or prove recovery complete.

This matches the inspected deployment consumer: supplied keys are installed through `add_full_access_key`, while registry-role changes operate on RBAC membership. [NEAR’s access-key documentation](https://docs.near.org/protocol/accounts-contracts/access-keys#full-access-keys) independently confirms that full-access keys can add/remove keys and replace contract code.

Maintenance safeguards continue to reuse existing role management, approval dispatch, registration callbacks, migration handles, and upgrade primitives. The inspected approval and reservation consumers have explicit handling rather than silently discarding those values.
