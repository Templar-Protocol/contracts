## Prior blocker disposition

1. **Additional unauthorized child keys: resolved and preserved.** Line **133** requires re-enumerating child keys during recovery, removing **every unauthorized key** through a trusted full-access key on that child where available, and verifying the final key list. It explicitly states that rotating Deployer does not revoke existing child keys.

2. **Critic’s P2—attacker-modified child code/state: resolved.** Line **133** now requires: “Before resuming use, establish trusted child code and state.” This makes integrity a separate recovery gate rather than treating successful key removal as evidence of restoration. The fallback explicitly applies when integrity cannot be established or safely restored, **even if a trusted child key remains**. That covers the previously missed case where the attacker altered code or storage before losing access.

3. **No trusted child key or authorized removal method: resolved and preserved.** Line **133** directs operators to stop using that Deployer and apply the same disclosure/migration fallback. It does not invent registry-Owner authority over an existing child or assume that an authorized removal route exists.

4. **Migration and keyless-market limitations: preserved.** Unrecoverable integrity requires disclosure and user migration to a **newly reviewed market where feasible**. The keyless-market limitation remains explicit; the record does not promise restoration of compromised funds or unconditional migration feasibility.

5. **Earlier post-key-deletion proof: remains resolved.** Decision **6**, lines **70–77**, retains both pre-deletion DAO proofs and mandatory post-deletion same-code upgrade plus distinct Uploader→DAO approval→Deployer deployment, with receipt and key inspection before alpha→v1 promotion.

## Tradeoff assessment

The correction strengthens recovery requirements without reopening settled design choices:

- Conventional Owner behavior, including two-step transfer and renunciation, remains separate from explicit Uploader/Deployer membership.
- Borsh pre-funded uploads and original-payer pending/retained charges remain selected, with their funding and accounting burden acknowledged.
- Pending-only duplicates preserve approved aliases without a historical hash index or scans.
- Handle-moving migration avoids rewriting historical entries.
- Protocol reserves and shortfall-safe cleanup remain included; administrative withdrawal, forced unregister, Owner→Admin migration, and entry-rewriting migration remain rejected.
- Production execution, gateway/tmplrmgr UX, human acceptance, and implementation merge remain deferred.

## Security and maintenance assessment

The corrected recovery model distinguishes three independent concerns: removing unauthorized access, replacing compromised deployment authority, and establishing trusted child code/state. A clean key list or surviving trusted key is no longer sufficient to resume operation.

This matches the separate implementation’s consuming path: `deploy` accepts supplied keys and installs each through `add_full_access_key` (`contract/registry/src/lib.rs:624–633,720–726`). Registry role changes do not revoke those child-account keys. [NEAR’s access-key documentation](https://docs.near.org/protocol/accounts-contracts/access-keys#full-access-keys) confirms that full-access keys can deploy replacement contracts and add or remove keys.

The ADR appropriately states the required recovery outcome without introducing an unreviewed restoration mechanism. Where that outcome cannot be established safely, migration remains conditional rather than a guarantee.

## Requirement coverage

- **Authority:** Conventional Owner behavior, including two-step transfer and renunciation, remains intentional. Explicit Uploader/Deployer membership is separate from Owner’s action bypass. Retained registry full-access keys remain an expressly disclosed independent control path.
- **Upload and accounting:** Borsh uploads use NEP-145 pre-funding. Pending and retained charges remain associated with the original payer; released credit is payer-withdrawable. Administrative withdrawal and forced unregistration remain excluded.
- **Approval and compatibility:** Owner hash-only Uploaded/Global approval, legacy registration, permanent successful version keys, and retryable failed probes remain covered. Pending-only duplicate rejection permits approved aliases without historical hash scans or an index.
- **Migration and solvency:** O(1) collection-handle migration preserves history and Owner slots. Protocol reserves, deployment-record reservations, shortfall-safe failure cleanup, and atomic V2 upgrade checking remain included.
- **Recovery and rollout:** All-key cleanup and code/state integrity are separate requirements. Both pre- and post-key-deletion proofs remain mandatory.
- **Scope:** Production execution, gateway/tmplrmgr UX, human acceptance, and implementation merge remain deferred. Owner→Admin migration and historical entry rewriting remain rejected.

## Alternative fairness

The alternatives remain substantive and proportionate. Owner-only/deposit-based operation offers fewer interfaces but retains concentrated operational authority and less explicit retained-payer accounting. Replacing Owner with Admin adds migration without improving the selected capability separation; historical hash bans/indexes impose different alias semantics and history-management costs; forced unregistration/admin withdrawal conflict with payer ownership; entry rewriting adds migration risk without being necessary.

The selected design acknowledges its own costs: accounting complexity, payer top-ups, protocol-owned backing, and trusted deployment operators. The recovery correction does not claim those trust costs have disappeared.
