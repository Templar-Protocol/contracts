## Prior blocker disposition

References below are to the candidate ADR.

1. **Additional attacker-controlled Deployer grants — resolved, lines 145–147.** After containing compromised Owner authority, recovery must enumerate **explicit Deployer membership** and revoke **every unauthorized grant** through `set_role`, expressly including the compromised account’s grant, before any child-key removal. Revoking only the initially identified account no longer satisfies the procedure.

2. **Owner bypass containment — preserved, lines 141–144.** Owner must be secured or transferred before child-key cleanup. If that is impossible, affected children must stop being used, with disclosure and feasible user migration rather than treating Deployer revocation as sufficient containment.

3. **Previously authorized deployments — preserved, lines 148–149.** Deployment receipts and callbacks authorized before revocation must settle before enumeration and cleanup. The procedure does not assume revocation cancels already-dispatched work.

4. **Post-compromise children and additional child keys — preserved, lines 150–151.** Recovery includes children created after the compromise and requires removal of **every unauthorized key**, using trusted full-access authority on the affected child where available.

5. **Replacement and integrity gates — preserved, lines 152–154.** A reviewed replacement Deployer is granted only after the key list is clean. Trusted child **code and state**, not merely a clean credential list, must be established before use resumes.

6. **Unrecoverable integrity or removal authority — preserved, lines 155–159.** Disclosure and feasible migration remain required when integrity cannot be established or safely restored—even if a trusted key survives—or when no authorized removal path exists. The keyless-market limitation remains explicit.

## Tradeoff assessment

The correction preserves the selected architecture:

- Conventional Owner administration remains separate from explicit Uploader/Deployer membership.
- Borsh uploads use NEP-145 pre-funding, with pending and retained storage attributable to the original payer.
- Owner hash-only approval retains legacy registration and callback compatibility.
- Pending-only uniqueness permits approved aliases without historical hash bans, indexes, or scans.
- V1→V2 migration moves collection handles rather than rewriting history.
- Protocol reserves, shortfall-safe cleanup, and pre-/post-key-deletion DAO proofs remain required.

The ADR acknowledges payer top-ups, protocol-owned backing, accounting complexity, and trusted deployment operators. Administrative withdrawal, forced unregistration, Owner→Admin migration, and historical-entry rewriting remain rejected. Production execution, gateway/tmplrmgr UX, human acceptance, and implementation merge remain deferred.

## Security and maintenance assessment

The revised sequence correctly separates **Owner containment → removal of all unauthorized deployment grants → settlement of in-flight work → child credential cleanup → integrity verification**.

The pinned implementation supports these distinctions. `assert_role` accepts either current Owner authority or explicit membership; `set_role` is Owner-only; and `list_role` enumerates explicit members. Ownership transfer therefore cannot substitute for removing attacker-controlled grants. Conversely, role revocation does not remove keys already installed on children.

The deployment producer and its completion consumer were both inspected: `deploy` installs supplied full-access keys and schedules `deploy_01_finalize`; that callback finalizes deployment records and accounting without re-authorizing the original Deployer. Waiting for previously authorized work is therefore necessary.

The existing setter supports removing the last explicit member with `set:false` and `allow_removing_last_member:true`, as documented in the rollout procedure. Containment does not require prematurely granting a replacement. No new API, compatibility shim, or recovery mechanism is introduced.

## Architect finding disposition

1. **Delegated attacker grants — resolved.** ADR lines **145–147** require enumerating explicit Deployer membership and revoking **every unauthorized grant**, including the compromised account’s, after Owner containment and before child-key removal. This no longer assumes that revoking the original account removes grants it created.

2. **Owner bypass — preserved.** Lines **141–144** require securing or transferring compromised Owner authority first, with stop-use, disclosure, and feasible migration if that cannot be achieved. This matches the implementation’s separate Owner bypass: removing explicit Deployer membership alone does not remove Owner authority (`lib.rs:186–199`).

3. **Recovery ordering and integrity gates — preserved.** Lines **148–159** retain settlement of previously authorized deployments, enumeration of post-compromise children and keys, removal of every unauthorized key using trusted child authority, replacement only after a clean key list, and verification of trusted code **and state** before resuming use. Disclosure/migration remains required when integrity cannot be restored or removal authority is unavailable, including the keyless-market limitation.

The diff against `8ea978c2` changes only recovery step 2. Architect 10’s preservation claims therefore agree with both the complete corrected ADR and the narrow correction.

| Trigger | Consequence under the corrected ADR |
|---|---|

## Alternative fairness

The alternatives receive substantive treatment rather than dismissal. Owner-only/deposit-based operation offers fewer interfaces but retains concentrated authority and less explicit retained-payer accounting. Replacing Owner with Admin adds migration without improving the selected separation. Historical hash bans/indexes restrict aliases and introduce history-management costs; forced unregistration or administrative withdrawal conflicts with payer-owned credit; entry rewriting increases migration risk.

The selected design acknowledges its own costs: accounting complexity, payer top-ups, protocol backing, and trusted deployment operators. It does not claim that role separation guarantees immutable or keyless children.
