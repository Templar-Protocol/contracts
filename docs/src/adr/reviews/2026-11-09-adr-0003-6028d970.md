## Prior blocker disposition

References below are to the candidate ADR.

1. **Recovery presentation and replacement ordering — resolved.** The failure-table row at line **137** now points to the numbered procedure at **139–153**. Step 1 requires `set_role` revocation before child-key removal; step 4 permits a reviewed replacement Deployer grant only **after the key list is clean**. 2. **In-flight deployment draining — resolved.** Steps 2–3 at **142–145** require previously authorized deployment receipts and callbacks to settle **before re-enumeration and cleanup**. The keyless-market limitation remains explicit.

## Tradeoff assessment

The clarifications preserve the selected architecture:

- Conventional Owner administration and explicit Uploader/Deployer membership remain separate.
- Borsh uploads use NEP-145 pre-funding; pending and retained storage remain attributable to the original payer.
- Owner hash-only approval retains legacy registration and callback compatibility.
- Pending-only uniqueness permits approved aliases without historical hash bans, indexes, or scans.
- V1→V2 migration moves collection handles rather than rewriting history.
- Protocol reserves, shortfall-safe cleanup, and pre-/post-key-deletion DAO proofs remain required.

The ADR acknowledges the resulting costs: payer top-ups, protocol-owned backing, accounting complexity, and trusted deployment operators. Administrative withdrawal, forced unregistration, Owner→Admin migration, and historical-entry rewriting remain rejected. Production execution, gateway/tmplrmgr UX, human acceptance, and implementation merge remain deferred.

## Security and maintenance assessment

The recovery procedure correctly separates **authority containment, asynchronous deployment completion, credential cleanup, and child-integrity restoration**.

In the pinned implementation, `deploy` checks Deployer authority before constructing child-account actions, installs supplied keys through `add_full_access_key`, and schedules `deploy_01_finalize`. The callback handles deployment completion and accounting; it does not revoke child credentials or re-authorize the original Deployer. Consequently, role revocation cannot substitute for draining previously authorized work, removing child keys, or restoring trusted code/state.

The ADR does not invent registry-Owner authority over existing children. Direct cleanup requires a trusted child full-access key or another authorized removal method. The implementation and rollout documentation also support revoking the last explicit role member through `allow_removing_last_member:true`; granting a replacement first is not necessary.

The changes add no new recovery API, compatibility shim, or implementation mechanism. Reusing existing role management and callback paths keeps the decision maintainable while making its operational trust boundaries explicit.

## Alternative fairness

The alternatives receive substantive treatment. Owner-only/deposit-based operation offers fewer interfaces but retains concentrated authority and less explicit retained-payer accounting. Replacing Owner with Admin adds migration without improving the selected capability separation. Historical hash bans/indexes restrict aliases and introduce history-management costs; administrative withdrawal/forced unregistration conflict with payer-owned credit; entry rewriting increases migration risk.

The selected design acknowledges its own costs: accounting complexity, payer top-ups, protocol-owned backing, and trusted deployment operators. The correction does not imply that role separation makes children immutable or eliminates operational trust.

## Security, maintenance, and verification assessment

The recovery model separates **containment of deployment authority**, **removal of unauthorized child credentials**, and **restoration or proof of child integrity**. The new settlement step closes the asynchronous gap between containment and enumeration.

Approval dispatch, deployment-reservation finalization, failed-global cleanup, atomic upgrade construction, and mock-DAO FunctionCall/outcome handling were inspected rather than inferred from producer APIs alone.

Maintenance remains bounded through reuse of conventional Owner/RBAC behavior, existing approval/probe paths, callback compatibility, and handle-moving migration.
